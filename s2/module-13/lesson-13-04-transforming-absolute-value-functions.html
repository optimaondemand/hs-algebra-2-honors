📚 VOCABULARY

Compression (vertical or horizontal): A transformation that shrinks the graph of a function either toward the x-axis (vertical compression, \( |k| < 1 \) in \(k \cdot f(x)\) \)) or toward the y-axis (horizontal compression, \( |k| > 1 \) in \( f(kx) \)).

Horizontal Translation: A transformation that shifts the graph of a function left or right along the x-axis without changing its shape; produced by replacing \( x \) with \( x - h \) in the equation.

Parameter: A constant in a function's equation whose value controls a specific feature or behavior of the graph; in \( f(x) = a|x - h| + k \), the parameters are \( a \), \( h \), and \( k \).

Parent Function: The simplest, unmodified version of a function family used as the starting point for describing transformations; for the absolute value family, the parent function is \( f(x) = |x| \).

Reflection: A transformation that flips the graph of a function across an axis; multiplying \( f(x) \) by a negative value reflects it across the x-axis, while replacing \( x \) with \( -x \) reflects it across the y-axis.

Stretch (vertical or horizontal): A transformation that pulls the graph of a function away from the x-axis (vertical stretch, \( |a| > 1 \)) or away from the y-axis (horizontal stretch, \( 0 < |k| < 1 \) in \( f(kx) \)).

Transformation: A change applied to a function that alters the position, orientation, or shape of its graph while preserving the overall function family.

Vertical Translation: A transformation that shifts the graph of a function up or down along the y-axis without changing its shape; produced by adding a constant \( k \) to \( f(x) \).

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how the graph of an absolute value function changes when we apply transformations — shifting it up, down, left, or right, stretching or compressing it, and reflecting it across an axis. In the previous lesson, you studied the key features of absolute value graphs: vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, domain, range, and intervals of increase and decrease. Today you will connect each of those features directly to the equation by understanding exactly what each part of \( f(x) = a|x - h| + k \) does to the parent graph. You will learn to identify a transformation and its value from an equation, a graph, or a table of values; describe the combined effect when two transformations act together; and produce the equation, table, or graph of a transformed function given the parent and a transformation rule. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to read any equation in the family of absolute value functions and predict its graph — or work in reverse, reading a graph and writing its equation.

Parent Function

Every transformation in this lesson is measured as a change from the parent function \( f(x) = |x| \). Before analyzing transformations, you need to know this function well — its equation, its table of values, and its graph — because you will compare every transformed function against it. The vertex \( (0, 0) \) is the most important reference point: it tells you where the function is centered, and it is the first thing that moves (or stays put) when a transformation is applied.

Three Things to Watch When Comparing to the Parent

1. Where is the vertex? In the parent, it sits at \( (0, 0) \). If the vertex moved, a translation occurred. How far and in which direction tells you the type and value.

2. How steep are the arms? In the parent, each arm has a rate of change of \( \pm 1 \). If the arms are steeper, a vertical stretch occurred. If flatter, a compression. If the arms match the parent but are in the opposite direction, a reflection occurred.

3. Are the y-values in the table the same as the parent, just in different rows? That signals a horizontal translation. Are the y-values scaled by a constant factor? That signals a vertical stretch or compression.

 

The Four Transformation Types

When a real number is added to or multiplied by the input or output of \( f(x) = |x| \), the graph changes in a predictable way. The table below summarizes all four types you will work with.

Transformation Reference for \( f(x) = |x| \)

\( f(x) + k \) — Vertical Translation: Adding \( k \) to the output shifts every point up (\( k > 0 \)) or down (\( k < 0 \)) by \( |k| \) units. Vertex moves from \( (0,0) \) to \( (0, k) \). In the table, all y-values change by \( +k \); x-values stay the same.

\( f(x + k) \) — Horizontal Translation: Adding \( k \) inside the function shifts the graph left when \( k > 0 \) and right when \( k < 0 \). Vertex moves from \( (0,0) \) to \( (-k, 0) \). In the table, the same y-values appear but at x-values shifted by \( -k \).

\( k \cdot f(x) \) — Vertical Stretch or Compression: Multiplying the output by \( k \) scales all y-values. When \( |k| > 1 \), the arms get steeper (stretch). When \( 0 < |k| < 1 \), the arms get flatter (compression). If \( k \) is negative, the graph also reflects over the x-axis.

\( {-f(x)} \) — Reflection over the x-axis: A special case of \( k \cdot f(x) \) where \( k = -1 \). All y-values are negated; the V flips upside down and opens downward. The vertex stays at the same location but becomes a maximum instead of a minimum.


Why does \( f(x+k) \) with \( k > 0 \) shift the graph to the LEFT? The vertex of \( f(x) = |x| \) occurs where the inside equals zero — at \( x = 0 \). In \( f(x + 3) = |x + 3| \), the inside equals zero when \( x + 3 = 0 \), i.e., \( x = -3 \). The vertex shifted left 3. Adding a positive \( k \) inside moves the whole graph in the negative x-direction.



Identifying From an Equation

Compare the given function to the parent \( f(x) = |x| \) and match it to one of the four transformation forms.

Given: \( g(x) = |x - 4| \)

Step 1 — Compare to parent: The parent has just \( x \) inside the bars. The given function has \( x - 4 \) inside the bars. The outside is unchanged.

Step 2 — Match the form: This is \( f(x + k) \) with \( x + k = x - 4 \), so \( k = -4 \).

Step 3 — Determine type and direction: Horizontal translation. Since \( k = -4 < 0 \), the graph shifts right by \( 4 \) units.

Step 4 — Confirm with vertex: Set \( x - 4 = 0 \to x = 4 \). New vertex: \( (4, 0) \). It moved right 4 from the origin. ✓

Answer:

Transformation type: Horizontal translation   |   Direction: Right   |   Value: \( k = -4 \)   |   New vertex: \( (4, 0) \)


Identifying From a Graph

Compare the transformed graph to the parent \( f(x) \) (shown dashed). Look at where the vertex moved, whether the arms changed steepness, and whether the graph flipped direction.

Step 1 — Compare vertices: The parent vertex is at \( (0, 0) \). The transformed vertex is at \( (0, 3) \). The x-coordinate is the same; the y-coordinate increased by 3.

Step 2 — Check the arms: Both graphs have the same steepness — the arms are not wider or narrower. No stretch or compression occurred.

Step 3 — Identify the form: The vertex moved straight up 3 units, matching \( f(x) + k \) with \( k = 3 \).

Answer:

Transformation type: Vertical translation up   |   Value: \( k = 3 \)   |   Equation: \( g(x) = f(x) + 3 = |x| + 3 \)

Combined Effects of Two Transformations

When a function includes more than one transformation, analyze each part of the equation independently. Changes outside the absolute value bars affect the output (y-values) and produce vertical translations, stretches, compressions, or reflections. Changes inside the absolute value bars affect the input (x-values) and produce horizontal translations. When both types appear together, both effects occur simultaneously on the graph. For each problem below, select the answer you believe is correct, then expand the solution to check and read the explanation.

Example 6

The function \( g(x) = f(x - 3) + 2 \) represents a transformation of \( f(x) = |x| \). Which statement best describes the combined effect on the graph?

(A)   Horizontal shift left 3 units and vertical shift up 2 units

(B)   Horizontal shift right 3 units and vertical shift up 2 units

(C)   Horizontal shift right 3 units and vertical shift down 2 units

(D)   Vertical stretch by 3 and vertical shift up 2 units

Show Solution

Inside the bars: \( x - 3 \) matches \( f(x + k) \) with \( k = -3 \). Since \( k < 0 \), the graph shifts right 3 units.

Outside the bars: \( + 2 \) is added to the output, matching \( f(x) + k \) with \( k = 2 \). Since \( k > 0 \), the graph shifts up 2 units.

Combined: The vertex moves from \( (0,0) \) to \( (3, 2) \). Equation: \( g(x) = |x - 3| + 2 \).

Answer:

(B)   Horizontal shift right 3 units and vertical shift up 2 units ✓



Example 7

The function \( g(x) = -2 \cdot f(x) \) represents a transformation of \( f(x) = |x| \). Which statement best describes the combined effect on the graph?

(A)   Reflection over the y-axis and vertical stretch by factor 2

(B)   Vertical compression by factor 2 and horizontal shift right

(C)   Reflection over the x-axis and vertical stretch by factor 2

(D)   Horizontal compression by factor 2 and reflection over x-axis

Show Solution

Analyze \( k = -2 \): The factor \( -2 \) is applied to the entire output. Break it into two parts:

The negative sign (\( k = -1 \)) negates all y-values → reflection over the x-axis (graph opens downward).

The absolute value of the factor (\( |k| = 2 > 1 \)) scales all y-values by 2 → vertical stretch by factor 2 (arms are steeper).

Combined: \( g(x) = -2|x| \). The graph opens downward with arms twice as steep as the parent.

Answer:

(C)   Reflection over the x-axis and vertical stretch by factor 2 ✓



Example 8

The function \( g(x) = \dfrac{1}{2} f(x + 4) \) represents a transformation of \( f(x) = |x| \). Which statement best describes the combined effect on the graph?

(A)   Horizontal shift right 4 units and vertical compression by factor \( \frac{1}{2} \)

(B)   Vertical stretch by factor 2 and horizontal shift left 4 units

(C)   Vertical compression by factor \( \frac{1}{2} \) and horizontal shift left 4 units

(D)   Horizontal compression by factor 2 and horizontal shift left 4 units

Show Solution

Inside the bars: \( x + 4 \) matches \( f(x + k) \) with \( k = 4 \). Since \( k > 0 \), the graph shifts left 4 units.

Outside the bars: \( \frac{1}{2} \) multiplies the entire output. Since \( 0 < \frac{1}{2} < 1 \), this is a vertical compression — the arms become flatter.

Combined: \( g(x) = \frac{1}{2}|x + 4| \). The vertex moves to \( (-4, 0) \) and the arms are half as steep as the parent.

Answer:

(C)   Vertical compression by factor \( \frac{1}{2} \) and horizontal shift left 4 units ✓



Creating a Corresponding Table, Graph, or Equation

Create the table, graph, and equation for \( g(x) = f(x) - 2 \)

Since it is outside of the function, this affects the outputs and will be added to the outside of the absolute value. All y-values will shift down in the graph and 2 will be subtracted from all y-values in the table.

Equation: \( g(x) = f(x) - 2 = |x| - 2 \) (subtract 2 from every y-value of the parent)

Table: Keep all x-values the same. Subtract 2 from each f(x) value.

Key features: Vertex at \( (0, -2) \). X-intercepts at \( (\pm 2, 0) \). Y-intercept at \( (0, -2) \). Range: \( [-2, \infty) \).

Conclusion

In this lesson, you explored the full set of transformations that can be applied to the absolute value parent function \( f(x) = |x| \). You learned that adding to or subtracting from the output produces vertical translations, while making changes inside the function to the input produces horizontal translations — and that these two types behave in distinctly different ways. Multiplying the output by a factor greater than 1 stretches the graph vertically, while a factor between 0 and 1 compresses it, and a negative factor introduces a reflection across the x-axis. You practiced identifying each transformation from three different representations — an equation, a graph, and a table of values — and you learned to connect the value of \( k \) to the type and magnitude of the change. You then combined multiple transformations and described their effects together, and finally, you applied transformation rules to produce the equation, table, and graph of a transformed function from the parent. All of these skills come together in the general form \( g(x) = a|x - h| + k \), where \( a \) controls the direction and steepness, \( h \) controls the horizontal position of the vertex, and \( k \) controls the vertical position — giving you complete, efficient control over the shape and location of any absolute value graph from its equation alone.
