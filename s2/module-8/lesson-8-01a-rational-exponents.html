📚 VOCABULARY

Base: The number or variable that is being multiplied repeatedly in an exponential expression.

Coefficient: A numerical factor multiplied by a variable or algebraic expression.

Equivalent expressions: Expressions that may look different but have the same value for all values of the variables.

Exponent: The number that tells how many times the base is used as a factor.

Exponent properties: Rules that govern how to manipulate expressions involving exponents, such as product, quotient, and power properties.

Monomial: A single term consisting of a number, a variable, or the product of numbers and variables with non-negative integer exponents.

Natural base (e): An irrational number approximately equal to 2.71828, often used as the base of natural exponential functions.

Power rule: A property stating that when a power is raised to another power, multiply the exponents.

Product rule: A property stating that when multiplying expressions with the same base, add the exponents.

Quotient rule: A property stating that when dividing expressions with the same base, subtract the exponents.

Rational exponent: An exponent written as a fraction, where the numerator represents a power, and the denominator represents a root.

Simplify: To rewrite an expression in its most reduced or efficient form while retaining the same value.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In Algebra 2, you will work with increasingly complex exponential expressions, including variable expressions that contain rational (fractional) exponents. Understanding how to manipulate these expressions is essential for working with functions, modeling growth and decay, and simplifying algebraic expressions. The laws of exponents allow you to rewrite expressions in equivalent forms by using consistent patterns. In this lesson, you will learn how to apply the product rule, quotient rule, and power rule to expressions with variable bases and rational exponents. You will read explanations, examine examples, and then try problems where you generate equivalent expressions using these properties.

Laws of Exponents

Product Rule When multiplying expressions with the same base, add the exponents.
Quotient Rule When dividing expressions with the same base, subtract the exponents.
Power Rule When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.
Powers of Product Rule When two bases that are multiplied are raised to the same base, the power can be distributed.
Powers of Quotient Rule When two bases that are being divided are raised to the same base, the power can be distributed.

Conclusion

The laws of exponents allow you to simplify and rewrite expressions efficiently, even when variables have rational exponents. By using the product rule, quotient rule, and power rule, you can transform expressions into clearer equivalent forms and prepare them for solving equations or analyzing functions. These exponent rules are foundational tools that you will continue applying throughout Algebra 2, Precalculus, and Calculus. As you practice, focus on recognizing patterns and choosing the rule that fits the structure of the expression.
