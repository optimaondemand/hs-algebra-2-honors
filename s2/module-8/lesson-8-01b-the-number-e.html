📚 VOCABULARY

Base: The number or variable that is being multiplied repeatedly in an exponential expression.

Coefficient: A numerical factor multiplied by a variable or algebraic expression.

Equivalent expressions: Expressions that may look different but have the same value for all values of the variables.

Exponent: The number that tells how many times the base is used as a factor.

Exponent properties: Rules that govern how to manipulate expressions involving exponents, such as product, quotient, and power properties.

Monomial: A single term consisting of a number, a variable, or the product of numbers and variables with non-negative integer exponents.

Natural base (e): An irrational number approximately equal to 2.71828, often used as the base of natural exponential functions.

Power rule: A property stating that when a power is raised to another power, multiply the exponents.

Product rule: A property stating that when multiplying expressions with the same base, add the exponents.

Quotient rule: A property stating that when dividing expressions with the same base, subtract the exponents.

Rational exponent: An exponent written as a fraction, where the numerator represents a power and the denominator represents a root.

Simplify: To rewrite an expression in its most reduced or efficient form while retaining the same value.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In Algebra 2, you learn about many types of exponential functions, including ones that model real-world growth and decay. One of the most important numbers in all of mathematics is the constant e, sometimes called the natural base. Just like \(\pi\) appears everywhere in geometry, the number e appears naturally in situations involving continuous growth, interest, populations, and even probability. In this lesson, you will learn what the number e represents, how to evaluate expressions involving e using rational exponents, and how to simplify algebraic expressions with base e using the laws of exponents.

Evaluating Expressions with Base \(e\)

When you evaluate an expression involving e, treat it like any other base. The difference is that you will use a calculator to approximate its value.

Most calculators (and phone calculator apps) have a button for e, as well as a button for eˣ.

Example 1

Evaluate the expression. Round to the nearest thousandths.

\(e^3\)

Solution:

We will use the calculator to evaluate the expression.

Now, we will round the value off to 3 decimal places for the thousandths.

Answer: \(e^3\approx 20.086\)



Example 2

Evaluate the expression. Round to the nearest tenths.

\(e^{\frac{3}{5}}\)

Solution:

We will use the calculator to evaluate the expression.

Now, we will round the value off to 1 decimal place for the tenths.

Answer: \(e^{\frac{3}{5}}\approx 1.8\)



Example 3

Evaluate the expression. Round to the nearest hundredths.

\(e^{-3}\)

Solution:

We will use the calculator to evaluate the expression.

Now, we will round the value off to 2 decimal places for the hundredths.

Answer: \(e^{-3}\approx 0.05\)

 

Laws of Exponents with \(e\)

The laws of exponents apply to exponential expressions with base \(e\), as they do to other bases.

Product Rule When multiplying expressions with the same base, add the exponents.
Quotient Rule When dividing expressions with the same base, subtract the exponents.
Power Rule When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.

Example 1

Generate an equivalent expression.

\(e^{-7}\cdot e^{12}\)

Solution:

Since the bases are being multiplied, we apply the product rule and add the exponents.

Answer: \(e^5\)



Example 2

Generate an equivalent expression.

\(\Large \frac{e^{5/2}}{e^{1/2}}\)

Solution:

Since the bases are being divided, we apply the quotient rule and subtract the exponents.

Answer: \(e^2\)



Example 3

Generate an equivalent expression.

\(\Large (e^{2/3})^6\)

Solution:

Since the base raised to a power is being raised to another power, we apply the power rule and multiply the exponents.

Answer: \(e^4\)



Example 4

Generate an equivalent expression.

\(\Large \frac{e^{3/2}\cdot e^{1/6}}{e^{5/3}}\)

Solution:

We will begin by applying the product rule to the two expressions in the numerator to combine them to one by adding the powers. We will need to get a common denominator to add the fractional powers.

Next, we will combine the numerator and denominator by subtracting the powers. Again, we will need to get a common denominator.

Answer: \(1\)



Conclusion

The constant e plays a central role in higher mathematics because it naturally arises in situations involving continuous change. Understanding how to evaluate expressions with base e and how to simplify algebraic expressions using exponent laws prepares you for work with exponential functions, logarithms, and eventually calculus. As you practice, focus on recognizing when exponent rules apply and how to combine or manipulate exponents correctly. Mastery of these skills will make working with exponential models much easier in future lessons.
