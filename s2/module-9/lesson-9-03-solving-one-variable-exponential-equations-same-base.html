📚 VOCABULARY

Base: The base is the number that is raised to a power in an exponential expression and represents the factor being repeatedly multiplied.

Equation: An equation is a mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal, connected by an equals sign.

Exponential Equation: An exponential equation is an equation in which a variable appears in the exponent.

Extraneous Solution: An extraneous solution is a value that appears to solve an equation during the solving process but does not satisfy the original equation when checked.

Natural Base (e): The natural base, \(e\), is an irrational number approximately equal to 2.718 that is used as the base of the natural exponential and natural logarithmic functions.

Simplify: To simplify means to rewrite an expression in a form that is easier to understand or work with while maintaining an equivalent value.

Solution: A solution is a value of the variable that makes an equation true when substituted into the equation.

Variable: A variable is a symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unknown or changing value in a mathematical expression or equation.

After solving an exponential equation, it is important to check the solution in the original equation. A solution is valid if it makes the original equation true.

In exponential equations, solutions are generally valid as long as the bases are positive and not equal to 1. However, checking is especially important when equations come from real-world contexts or when simplifying required multiple steps.

Example 1

A bacterial culture doubles every hour and is modeled by \(2^t=16\).

At what time will the culture reach a size of 16?

Solution:

We can rewrite 16 with a base 2.

Now, that the bases are the same, we can set the powers equal and solve for t.

We find that t=4. 

Answer: The culture reaches this size after 4 hours, which is reasonable in context.
