📚 VOCABULARY

Base: The fixed number greater than 0 and not equal to 1 that determines the exponential relationship in a logarithmic expression.

Condense: To combine multiple logarithms into a single logarithm using logarithmic properties.

Equivalent expression: An expression that may look different but represents the same value for all permissible values of the variable(s).

Expand: To rewrite a single logarithmic expression as a sum, difference, or multiple of logarithms using logarithmic properties.

Exponent: The number that indicates how many times the base is multiplied by itself.

Logarithm: The exponent to which a base must be raised to obtain a given number, called the argument.

Power property of logarithms: A rule that allows an exponent on the argument to be moved to the front of the logarithm as a coefficient, or vice versa.

Product property of logarithms: A rule stating that the logarithm of a product equals the sum of the logarithms of its factors.

Quotient property of logarithms: A rule stating that the logarithm of a quotient equals the difference of the logarithms of the numerator and denominator.

Rewrite: To express a mathematical expression in a different but equivalent form.

Simplify: To write an expression in its most concise and reduced form while preserving its value.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

Logarithms provide a powerful way to work with exponential relationships and complex expressions. Just as there are rules for simplifying expressions with exponents, logarithms follow specific laws that help us condense, expand, and rewrite expressions efficiently. In this lesson, you will focus on using the product, quotient, and power properties to manipulate algebraic logarithmic expressions. Mastering these skills will help prepare you for solving logarithmic equations and applying logarithms in real-world contexts.

Conclusion

The laws of logarithms allow you to move flexibly between different but equivalent forms of logarithmic expressions. By learning how to condense using the product and quotient properties, expand expressions into manageable pieces, and rewrite terms using the power property, you gain control over even complex algebraic logarithms. These skills are essential for solving logarithmic equations and for deeper work with exponential models in Algebra 2 and beyond.
