📚 VOCABULARY

Base: The base is the number that is raised to a power in an exponential or logarithmic expression and represents the factor being repeatedly multiplied.

Equation: An equation is a mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal, using an equals sign.

Exponential Equation: An exponential equation is an equation in which a variable appears in the exponent.

Extraneous Solution: An extraneous solution is a value that arises during the solving process but does not satisfy the original equation when checked.

Logarithm: A logarithm is the inverse of an exponential function and represents the exponent to which a base must be raised to produce a given number.

Natural Base (e): The natural base, \(e\), is an irrational number approximately equal to 2.718 that is commonly used as the base for natural exponential and logarithmic functions.

Natural Logarithm (ln): A natural logarithm is a logarithm with base \(e\), written as \(\ln{x}\), and is widely used in mathematics and science.

Power Property of Logarithms: The power property of logarithms states that the logarithm of a power is equal to the exponent multiplied by the logarithm of the base, written as \(\log_{b}{(x^k)}-k\cdot \log_{b}{x}\).

Solution: A solution is a value of the variable that makes an equation true when substituted into the original equation.

Variable: A variable is a symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unknown or changing value in a mathematical expression or equation.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In earlier lessons, you solved exponential equations by rewriting both sides with the same base. However, not all exponential equations can be rewritten this way. When the bases are different or cannot be matched easily, logarithms provide a powerful tool for solving these equations. In this lesson, you will learn how to apply logarithms and natural logarithms to solve exponential equations, how to use the power property of logarithms to isolate a variable, and how to solve equations involving the natural base e e e by taking the natural logarithm of both sides.

Power Property of Logarithms

We will also be using the power property of logarithms.

This property is essential when solving exponential equations because it allows the exponent (which contains the variable) to be moved to the front of the logarithm. Once the variable is no longer in the exponent, standard algebraic techniques can be used to isolate it.

In these situations, the variable representing time appears in the exponent, which means algebra alone is not enough to solve the equation. Instead, logarithms or natural logarithms are used to isolate the variable and find a solution.

Why Logarithms Are Necessary

In both compound and continuously compounded interest problems, the variable being solved for appears in the exponent. Logarithms allow you to:

  • Move the variable out of the exponent

  • Solve equations that cannot be rewritten with the same base

  • Interpret realistic time values in financial contexts

When the base is \(e\), use natural logarithms. When the base is not \(e\), you may use either \(\log\) or \(\ln\).



Conclusion

You practiced using the power property of logarithms to isolate variables and learned why taking the natural logarithm is especially effective when working with equations involving the base \(e\). These methods are essential tools for solving exponential models in Algebra 2 and for understanding real-world applications such as population growth, interest, and decay processes.
